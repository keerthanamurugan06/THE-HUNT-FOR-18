# THE HUNT FOR 18 – Investigation Storyline

## 1. Story Identity

**Event:** THE HUNT FOR 18  
**Tagline:** 18 Victims. 1 Killer. 1 Mystery. Are You Ready to Uncover the Truth?

THE HUNT FOR 18 is a continuous investigation challenge built around 18 victims and one unidentified killer.

Participants act as investigation teams. They must examine clues, solve challenges, recover evidence and connect information gathered across four rounds.

The investigation progresses from discovering individual victim-related information to understanding the larger case and identifying the killer.

## 2. Core Storyline Principle

The four rounds are parts of ONE investigation.

- Rounds 1–3 are preliminary rounds open to all teams; incomplete work in one never blocks participation in a later preliminary round.
- Rounds 1, 2 and 3 reveal six victims each, for 18 total. All discoveries persist on the same team-scoped Evidence Board.
- Each round contributes information required for the overall investigation.
- The optional, organizer-authorized final round brings the collected investigation together for the top six teams.
- The final objective is to identify the killer.

**Critical rule:** The final round must not introduce an unrelated mystery or change the established storyline.

## 3. Investigation Progression

### Round 1 – DIGITAL LOGIC TRAP

**Story purpose:** Initial investigation and evidence discovery.

Participants solve 9×9 Sudoku-based logic puzzles and their variants.

Each correctly solved puzzle unlocks one victim discovery; Round 1 reveals six total.

The evidence contributes to the investigation of the 18 victims and becomes useful in subsequent stages.

**Output:** Round 1 investigation evidence.

### Round 2 – PASSWORD BREAKER

**Story purpose:** Recover hidden investigation information.

Participants solve six independent password and code-breaking challenges.

Each correctly solved challenge reveals one hidden word and one victim. Round 2 reveals six total. The six words combine into an investigation-related key phrase.

One challenge requires evidence obtained in Round 1.

Correct phrase submission unlocks its defined investigation content, but does not gate Round 3 participation. Round access is organizer-controlled.

**Output:** Six hidden words, six victim discoveries and the phrase result.

### Round 3 — THE CRYPTO PROTOCOL

**Story purpose:** Recover and interpret three protected cryptographic message files connected to victims 13–18.

The challenges use repeating-key XOR with known plaintext, a multi-layer cipher/encoding path, and deliberately weak educational RSA. Participants use a bounded cryptography workbench to investigate and submit the recovered key and/or plaintext for server-side validation. No Python editor or external code execution is used.

Each independently completed challenge reveals two temporary victim placeholders and one protected code fragment. All discoveries persist to the same team-scoped Evidence Board. The fragments can be combined into a final unlock code; validating it does not activate Round 4.

Optional hints and configured deduction values are stored server-side. They do not change scores until the shared scoring system exists. Round access remains organizer-controlled, and earlier preliminary rounds do not block Round 3.

Exact competition questions, answer keys, hint content, marks, deduction values, and final code remain unfinalized. The current challenges are temporary demonstration content only.
### Round 4 – OPERATION: DEADLOCK

**Story purpose:** Connect the entire investigation and identify the killer.

If organizer-authorized, the top six teams enter a 2D pixel-art investigation maze. Round 4 remains locked by default and can be skipped if event time is insufficient; winners can then be declared using cumulative Round 1–3 scores.

The complete map is visible, but the routes are difficult to solve.

Wrong routes may contain mini-challenges before teams can return to the main progression.

Teams must use information collected from the earlier rounds to progress through the investigation and reach the final objective.

**Final objective:** Identify the killer by connecting the investigation evidence.

When Round 4 runs, finalists missing essential clues can recover them at evidence checkpoints. Recovered evidence does not award retroactive marks.

## 4. Continuity Rules

1. Maintain one continuous investigation across all four rounds.
2. Do not treat each round as a separate unrelated story.
3. Victim-related discoveries belong to Rounds 1–3.
4. The final round focuses on identifying the killer.
5. Earlier-round evidence must remain relevant to the final investigation.
6. Do not reveal confidential story details on the public event website.
7. Do not expose actual answers, evidence or hidden information before participants are authorized to access them.

## 5. Information Disclosure

### Public information

The public website may explain:
- The investigation theme.
- The event premise.
- The four round names.
- General skills participants will use.
- Event rules and participation details.

### Restricted information

The following must remain confidential:
- Victim identities and unreleased victim details.
- Killer identity.
- Actual clues and evidence.
- Puzzle solutions and answer keys.
- Hidden words and final key phrase.
- Final vault answer.
- Unreleased challenge mechanics.

### Organizer-only information

Organizers must be able to configure and manage confidential story content without exposing it to participants prematurely.

## 6. Storyline Completion Criteria

The investigation is considered complete when a qualified team:

1. Completes the investigation; any essential clues a finalist lacks can be recovered at an approved Round 4 checkpoint.
2. Reaches the final investigation objective.
3. Connects the required evidence.
4. Submits the killer identification through the authorized final-round interface.
5. Receives a server-validated result.

The exact evidence structure, final answer format and scoring rules must be documented separately before implementation.

## 7. Pending Story Decisions
